The central contribution of this paper is the CHASE-SQL framework: an ensemble that generates diverse SQL candidates via several prompting strategies and then selects a final query using a learned pairwise scoring model. Conceptually, this sits very close to MCS-SQL by Lee et al. (2024), which already proposes generating multiple candidate SQLs from diverse prompts and then performing a selection step to choose a final answer; C3 (Dong et al., 2023) and SQL-PaLM (Sun et al., 2023) likewise generate multiple diverse candidates and select among them using execution-based aggregation. Relative to this prior work, the submission’s main deltas are: (i) specific candidate-generation mechanisms (divide-and-conquer CoT, query-plan-based CoT, and online synthetic example generation), and (ii) replacing execution-based or prompted multiple-choice selection with a fine-tuned pairwise comparison classifier and win-count aggregation. These are nontrivial design choices and appear technically sound, but they refine an already-established “multi-path generation + selection” template rather than introduce a qualitatively new core mechanism. As such, while the paper offers a more engineered and possibly stronger instantiation of this paradigm, the central idea is too close to existing work, and the overall novelty is best characterized as mixed rather than a clear, standalone advance.
