The central contribution of this paper is a benchmark/framework for evaluating the role-playing abilities of language models using a three-agent, LM-driven setup (player, interrogator, and multiple judges) with dynamically generated queries and multi-model evaluation. Closest prior work is MORTISE in “Enhancing Role-playing Systems through Aggressive Queries: Evaluation and Improvement,” which already introduces a designed system to benchmark and stress-test role-playing LLMs via adversarial, role-relevant queries and an adversarial dataset for evaluation and improvement, directly overlapping with the submission’s goal of benchmarking role adherence. Other benchmarks such as SocialBench, RoleLLM/RoleBench, CharacterEval, and ECHO likewise already provide role-playing evaluation datasets and protocols, including multi-turn dialogues and automatic or GPT-based evaluators. Relative to these, the submission’s main deltas are operational design choices: making the benchmark fully dynamic (LM-generated interrogator prompts each run), structuring evaluation as a three-agent interaction, averaging multiple judge models to reduce bias, and using per-turn scalar scores with criteria like character consistency and entertainment. These are useful refinements but remain largely at the level of protocol engineering around an already-established task and setup. Overall, the central contribution is not novel enough to constitute a genuine advance.
