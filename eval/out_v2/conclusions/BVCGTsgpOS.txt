The paper’s central contribution is the FACTTEST framework: a distribution-free, finite-sample statistical testing procedure that uses a labeled calibration split and a score function to decide, with user-specified Type I error guarantees, when an LLM’s answer is factual and when it should abstain. Mohri et al. (2024) already propose essentially this core idea for LLM factuality: they use split conformal prediction on calibration scores, select an order-statistic (quantile) threshold, and obtain finite-sample, distribution-free control over the probability of incorrect outputs, yielding a selective-generation mechanism with abstention and the same type of user-specified error control. The present submission mainly reframes this machinery in Neyman–Pearson language, instantiates it with somewhat different scoring/labeling choices, and adds a theoretical power analysis plus an extension to covariate shift via density-ratio-based rejection sampling. While the covariate-shift extension and empirical results appear novel within the examined prior, they are secondary to the core method, which substantially overlaps with Mohri et al. (2024). Consequently, the central contribution is not novel enough relative to existing work, and the paper’s overall novelty is insufficient for a top-tier venue.
