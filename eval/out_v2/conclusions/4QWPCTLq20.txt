The submission’s central contribution is IntelLLM, a lightweight, training-free framework for KV‑cache compression that enables efficient long‑text inference via a combination of Center of Gravity Eviction (CGE), Remote Gap Localization (RGL), and a windowing mechanism. However, at the level of this central goal and problem formulation, there is already substantial prior work: GEAR (Zhang et al., 2024), LServe (Jin et al., 2024), RazorAttention (Gomez et al., 2024), Tactic (Gao et al., 2024), and R‑KV (Li et al., 2024) all propose no‑finetuning methods that compress or sparsify the KV cache at inference time to reduce memory/compute while preserving long‑context performance. What the present submission adds beyond these is a specific new eviction policy (CGE, masking the attention “center of gravity” and varying masking/normalization order) and a positional remapping strategy (RGL, shifting distant cache positions to preserve long‑range distinctions within a compression/nearest‑neighbor window design). These mechanisms appear technically distinct and are not found in the examined prior work, but they operate as alternative heuristics within an already well‑explored framework of training‑free KV compression for long‑context LLM serving. Given that this overarching framework is substantially anticipated by prior art, the paper’s novelty is, on balance, not strong enough for a top‑tier venue.
