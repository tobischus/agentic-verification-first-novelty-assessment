The paper’s central contribution is the SAFEPATCHING framework: deriving parameter-space “safety patches” and seamlessly integrating them into an already aligned LLM to improve safety while preserving utility. Bhardwaj et al. (2024) already introduce a very similar core idea in RESTA: compute a safety delta vector in parameter space and add it to a fine-tuned backbone to restore or improve safety, studying how operations on this delta affect safety–utility trade-offs. This substantially overlaps the submission’s main mechanism of parameter-patch addition for post-hoc safety alignment. The present work extends this paradigm by (i) deriving two complementary patches from harmful data via opposite gradient directions (one for safety enhancement, one for over-safety mitigation), and (ii) introducing a controllable integration scheme and a more “comprehensive” evaluation including continual PSA scenarios. However, these additions, while interesting, are incremental refinements on an existing weight-space safety-vector approach rather than a qualitatively new central mechanism. Since the core methodological idea—safety alignment via additive parameter patches to a pretrained/aligned model—is already delivered by Bhardwaj et al. (2024), the overall novelty of the submission is not sufficient for a top-tier venue.
