The paper’s central contribution is the CHASE-SQL framework: generating diverse SQL candidates via multiple prompting strategies and then selecting a single final query with a learned scoring model. At the framework level, this is substantially overlapped by MCS-SQL (Lee et al., 2024), which already proposes to leverage multiple prompts to produce diverse candidate SQLs and then apply a selection procedure to choose the best candidate, thereby improving Text-to-SQL performance. The present submission extends this idea by (i) instantiating several specific candidate generators (divide-and-conquer CoT, query-plan-based CoT, and online synthetic example generation) and (ii) replacing MCS-SQL’s execution-based filtering plus LLM multiple-choice selection with a trained pairwise comparison classifier that aggregates wins into a final score. While these additions are technically nontrivial, they are best viewed as alternative engineering choices within the same multi-candidate–plus–selection paradigm, rather than a qualitatively new mechanism. The pairwise selection agent and specific candidate generators are themselves not directly challenged, but they refine rather than fundamentally extend the core idea already realized in MCS-SQL. Consequently, the central contribution is not novel enough to meet the bar for a genuine advance.
