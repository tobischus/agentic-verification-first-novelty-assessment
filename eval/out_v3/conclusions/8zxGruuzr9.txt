The paper’s central contribution is the introduction of a “Value Anchor” prompting strategy that steers LLMs to answer PVQ‑RR items as a person strongly emphasizing a specified value, and the demonstration that this yields human‑like first- and second‑order value statistics. Prior work already gets quite close on several axes: Kovač et al. (2024), Hadar-Shoval et al. (2024), and Fischer et al. (2023) all administer Schwartz/PVQ-based instruments to LLMs and compare resulting value profiles to large human datasets, while Kang et al. (2023) injects target value distributions and prompts models on PVQ items. However, none of these works uses the specific “answer as a person that is [value]” Value Anchor persona framing, nor do they systematically study how such anchoring modifies the full correlation structure of values or analyze anchoring effects as a function of position on the Schwartz value circle. The additional explanation of how anchoring shapes correlations and the released multi-model persona datasets are likewise not directly covered by prior work. Because the core mechanism (Value Anchor prompting) and its demonstrated effect on human-consistent value correlations are not directly anticipated by existing studies, the paper’s novelty constitutes a genuine advance.
