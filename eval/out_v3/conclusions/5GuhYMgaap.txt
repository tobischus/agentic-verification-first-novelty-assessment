The paper’s central contribution is the SolverLearner framework, which aims to cleanly separate inductive from deductive reasoning via a two-step pipeline—Function Proposal by an LLM followed by Function Execution in an external interpreter so that the model does not perform the deductive step. However, this core mechanism is already realized in prior work. Chen et al. (2022) (Program of Thoughts) have LLMs generate executable programs and offload computation to external interpreters, explicitly “disentangling computation from reasoning,” while Pan et al. (2023) (Logic-LM) and “Hypothesis Search: Inductive Reasoning with Language Models” likewise use an LLM to induce a formal/programmatic representation from examples and then rely on a symbolic solver or Python interpreter for execution. These works thus already instantiate the same inductive–deductive split that SolverLearner claims as novel; the present paper’s main additions are a different framing (explicit inductive vs. deductive terminology, named stages) and specific experimental choices, which are peripheral rather than conceptual advances. The empirical finding that LLMs induce good rules but are weaker at rule execution is also substantially anticipated by “Phenomenal Yet Puzzling: Testing Inductive Reasoning Capabilities of Language Models with Hypothesis Refinement.” Overall, the novelty of the central contribution is insufficient.
