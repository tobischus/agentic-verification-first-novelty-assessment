The paper’s central contribution is FACTTEST, a statistical framework that evaluates LLM factuality and enables abstention with user-specified Type I error guarantees via a calibration split, a correctness score, and an order-statistic threshold. Mohri et al. (2024) already propose an almost identical core mechanism for factuality: a split conformal procedure that computes per-instance scores, selects a quantile-based threshold on a calibration set, and thereby delivers finite-sample, distribution-free control of the probability of incorrect outputs (Type I error), causing the model to abstain when scores fall below the threshold. The present submission primarily re-frames this as a Neyman–Pearson style classifier and applies it to QA, but the core idea—distribution-free, finite-sample factuality guarantees via conformal-style thresholding—is substantially overlapping rather than novel. The added covariate-shift extension based on density-ratio-based rejection sampling appears not to be covered in the examined prior work, but this is a secondary refinement on top of a borrowed core. Given that the main methodological advance is already delivered by Mohri et al. (2024), the overall novelty is insufficient for a top-tier venue despite some incremental extensions and empirical results.
