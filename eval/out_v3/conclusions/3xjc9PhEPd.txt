The paper’s central contribution is an empirical, multi-factor benchmark that studies how design choices for personalized LLMs (model family/size, PEFT variants, RAG, compression, data scale, and training time) interact under realistic resource constraints on edge devices, and distills these results into deployment guidelines. Closest prior work is spread across several narrower studies: Li et al. (2024) and Liu et al. (2024) examine quantization and on-device deployment but largely ignore personalization/customization trade-offs; Pu et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2024) analyze PEFT/LoRA vs other finetuning approaches and data/model scaling but not in an edge-specific, multi-model, compression-aware setting; Qin et al. (2023) and related works demonstrate on-device LoRA personalization but with fixed hyperparameters and without systematic comparisons to RAG or training-time budgets. Relative to these, the submission’s delta lies in unifying these axes into a single, large-scale experimental framework focused explicitly on constrained edge hardware, and in extracting concrete, actionable rules (e.g., LoRA vs RAG by task difficulty, near-universal LoRA rank/alpha defaults, 3–4 hour training budgets). This delta directly targets the paper’s core question and is not merely peripheral. Overall, the work represents a genuine advance in empirical understanding and practical guidance for edge LLM deployment.
