The paper’s central contribution is its claim that LLMs perform an intrinsic “detokenization” process in which subword sequences are combined into coherent whole-word representations, primarily in early-to-middle layers. Closely related work has already made this point: Lad et al. (2024) and Feucht et al. (2024) both show that multi-token lexical items are integrated into full-word/entity representations in early layers, using logit-lens-style layerwise analyses and probes to document a detokenization/erasure phenomenon. Relative to these, the submission adds more targeted and task-specific probes (contrasting words vs. nonwords, measuring layerwise retrieval of original words under controlled splits, and using Patchscopes-style generative interventions), but these are largely methodological refinements rather than a qualitatively new mechanistic story. Additional claims—that last subtokens can be mapped back to the original whole-word with high accuracy, that multi-token words form a latent internal lexicon recoverable from hidden states, and that FFN layers are central to detokenization—are either substantially overlapped by Feucht et al. (2024) or constitute narrower mechanistic detail on top of an already established phenomenon. Consequently, the incremental deltas over the closest prior work are peripheral, and the paper is not novel enough as a substantive advance.
