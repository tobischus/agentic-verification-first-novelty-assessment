The paper’s central contribution is the claim that LLMs internally “detokenize” subword sequences into coherent whole-word representations primarily in early-to-middle layers, and that multi-token words are represented in a latent internal lexicon that can be decoded from hidden states. Very similar conclusions are already established in Feucht et al. (2024) and Lad et al. (2024): both works show that multi-token lexical items undergo an early-layer detokenization/consolidation process, that last-token hidden states carry whole-word/multi-token information, and that many such multi-token items can be systematically recovered from early-layer representations, i.e., that the model supports a latent, tokenizer-agnostic vocabulary. The present submission adds more targeted probes and generative interventions (logit-lens retrieval curves, Patchscopes-style decoding, and word–nonword contrasts) and extends the analysis to explicitly implicate FFN layers, but these refinements are largely methodological and deepen an already established picture rather than changing it. While some secondary claims (e.g., precise reconstruction accuracy profiles and FFN-specific causal tests) appear novel, the core story—that LLMs perform early detokenization and maintain a latent vocabulary of multi-token words—is substantially anticipated by prior work. On balance, the paper is therefore not novel enough for a top-venue contribution.
