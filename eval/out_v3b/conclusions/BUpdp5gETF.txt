The paper’s central contribution is the introduction of decoupled Relative Learning Rate Schedules (RLRS) that assign distinct, cosine‑shaped learning‑rate schedules (via per‑component start/end multipliers) to different Transformer components, with a claimed benefit that these relative schedules can be tuned on small models and extrapolated to much larger ones. Closest prior work, Everett et al. (2024), already studies per‑layer learning‑rate prescriptions, uses cosine schedules, and explicitly demonstrates that learning‑rate hyperparameters tuned or derived on small models can transfer across widths up to tens of billions of parameters, directly challenging the submission’s claim about small‑to‑large hyperparameter extrapolation. LoRA+ by Hayou et al. (2024) likewise shows that using different learning rates for different parameter groups (LoRA A/B matrices) can substantially improve optimization and discusses scaling behavior. Relative to these, the present work mainly refines the parameterization (per‑component start/end multipliers on a cosine LR, including MoE‑specific modules) and offers empirical evaluations on dense and MoE Transformers. These deltas are method‐engineering choices and extensions of scope rather than a qualitatively new central mechanism. Overall, the novelty is mixed but, given that the core idea and extrapolation claim are substantially anticipated, not strong enough for a top‑tier venue.
