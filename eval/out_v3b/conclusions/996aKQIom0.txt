The paper’s central contribution is a benchmark/framework for evaluating role‑playing abilities of LLMs via a three‑agent setup (player, interrogator, judge) with LM‑driven, dynamic multi‑turn conversations and multi‑model judging. Zhang et al.’s “Enhancing Role-playing Systems through Aggressive Queries: Evaluation and Improvement” already introduces MORTISE, a designed system to benchmark and stress‑test role‑playing LLMs through adversarial, role‑relevant queries and an adversarial dataset, substantially overlapping with the submission’s core goal of benchmarking role‑playing performance. Other contemporaneous benchmarks such as SocialBench, ECHO (Ng et al., 2024), RoleLLM/RoleBench, and CharacterEval also provide concrete role‑play evaluation datasets, protocols, and automatic metrics, further covering the general idea of dedicated role‑play benchmarks with automatic assessment. Relative to this body of work, the submission’s main additions are operational: a particular three‑agent decomposition, on‑the‑fly LM‑generated questions to reduce memorization, multi‑judge score averaging, and a specific per‑turn scoring scheme over criteria like character consistency and entertainment. These design choices are useful but appear incremental rather than conceptually transformative over existing role‑play benchmarks and evaluation systems. On novelty grounds, the paper is therefore not novel enough for a top‑tier venue.
