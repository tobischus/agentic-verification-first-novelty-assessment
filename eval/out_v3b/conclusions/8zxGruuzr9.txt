The paper’s central contribution is the introduction of a “Value Anchor” prompting scheme that aims to induce human-like value correlations in LLMs when answering the PVQ-RR, and the empirical claim that this yields first- and second-order statistics closely mirroring human data. Prior work already covers several key pieces of this program: Kovač et al. (2024), Hadar-Shoval et al. (2024), and related work administer PVQ(-RR) to LLMs, simulate personas or contextual roles, and compare model-derived value profiles to large human PVQ datasets, while Kang et al. (2023) injects target value distributions and prompts LLMs to answer PVQ items. However, none of these papers implement the specific “answer as a person that is [value]” Value Anchor persona framing tied to an external value scale, nor do they directly target human-like value correlation structure as a primary outcome. The present submission’s novel delta lies in this particular anchoring prompt design plus a systematic analysis of how anchoring effects vary with conceptual distance on the Schwartz value circle. Given that the closest prior work overlaps methodologically but does not realize this specific mechanism or correlation-focused analysis, I judge the central contribution to be a genuine advance in novelty, not merely incremental.
